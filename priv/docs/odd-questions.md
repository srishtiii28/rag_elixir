# Questions people actually ask

## Can a dragon breathe fire on command?

No. Our dragons breathe fire when they sneeze, when they are startled, and
when lighting the café stove, which is their job. They don't breathe fire on
command, and asking them to is how sheds get burned down. Fire Cover doesn't
cover anything set on fire on purpose.

## Can I name the dragon?

No. Every dragon at the roost already has a name, and the dragons are fond of
them, even Captain Mopsy. You can call your dragon anything you like in your
head.

## Do dragons eat people?

No. Our dragons are fed before every hire and eat what the keepers give them:
mostly mutton, fish, turnips and, for Puddlewyrms, a great many apples. They
are not interested in eating riders. They are very interested in riders'
sandwiches.

## Can dragons talk?

Our dragons don't talk, but they understand far more than they let on,
especially their own names, the word "treat", and anything rude said about
them. Assume your dragon is listening.

## Can I keep a scale?

Only a moulted one. After the Great Moult, cleaned scales are sold in the roost
shop for 2 gold each. Please don't pull a scale off a dragon; it hurts, and the
dragon will remember your face.

## Can I bring my pet?

Small dogs can ride in a closed basket strapped to the saddle. Cats can't ride
any of our dragons, because dragons and cats have an old disagreement.
Horses should be stabled at the Windmill Inn, a mile down the hill, because
they don't like the smell of dragons.

## How old are the dragons?

Our dragons live for a very long time. Grandmother Ash is more than ninety.
Dumpling is about sixty. Pip and Kettle hatched two summers ago. We don't know
exactly how old the Thunderwings are, and the Thunderwings won't say.

## What if I need the toilet mid-flight?

Land at the nearest marked landing field. There is no other answer to this
question, and the dragons would like you to stop asking.

## Is it safe?

Dragon riding is as safe as we can make it. Every rider gets a safety
briefing, a helmet and goggles, a fireproof cloak, a whistle and a red flare,
and our rescue riders usually reach anyone who lights a flare within 45
minutes. Most accidents happen to riders who ignored the weather rules.
