# Flying safely

## Before you take off

Every rider gets a 10-minute safety briefing at the roost before take-off. A
keeper checks the saddle straps, your helmet and goggles, and that the whistle
and red flare are in your saddlebag. Then the keeper introduces you to your
dragon by name. Please use it.

To mount a dragon, wait for it to lower its shoulder, then climb up from the
left side. Never climb up from the right: dragons are left-handed and will
think you are trying to steal their treats. Riders who can't climb can use our
mounting ramp.

## Steering

To steer a dragon, lean the way you want to go and give two short taps on the
rein on that side. Pull both reins gently to slow down. To climb, lean back; to
come down, lean forward. Kicking a dragon does nothing, except annoy it.

One long blast on your whistle tells most dragons to land. Three short blasts
tell your dragon to come back to the roost, which is useful if you are lost,
because the dragon never is.

## Flight rules

Stay below the clouds at all times: dragons get lost in them and come out
sulking. Never fly over the royal palace, the cathedral or the Duke's
orchards. The Duke has a trebuchet and is not afraid to use it.

Griffin couriers carrying the royal post always have right of way. If you see
a griffin with a red satchel, give it room and let it pass. Don't race other
riders, don't fly closer than three dragon lengths to another dragon, and
don't fish from a dragon.

Only Mistgliders may fly after dark. Every other dragon must be back at the
roost by the second bell after sunset. Mistgliders must be back by the
midnight bell.

## Passengers and luggage

A Puddlewyrm carries one rider, a Ridgeback two, a Thunderwing up to four, and
a Mistglider one. A Ridgeback carries up to 40 pounds of luggage in
saddlebags. Don't carry anything shiny in an open bag: dragons hoard shiny
things, and they know where you keep them.

Small dogs can ride in a closed basket strapped to the saddle. Cats can't ride
any of our dragons: dragons and cats have had a disagreement for longer than
anyone can remember, and neither side will say what it was about.

## Weather

Puddlewyrms stay on the ground in any rain. Ridgebacks can fly in light rain
but not in storms. Thunderwings love storms, which is exactly why riders are
not allowed to fly them in one. If the weather turns, land at the nearest
landing field and wait it out.

Mistgliders don't fly in fog, because nobody, including the Mistglider, can
see a Mistglider in fog. In strong wind, keep below the treetops on a
Puddlewyrm and below the hilltops on anything else. Snow is fine for every
dragon except Puddlewyrms, who try to eat it.

If we cancel a hire because of a storm warning, you get a full refund or a
free new date.

## Feeding

Your dragon is fed before every hire, so it only needs the treats in your
bag. Never feed it anything else, and never feed it goat after 6pm: it will be
awake all night, and so will everyone within a mile. Dragons that ask for more
treats are lying.

Give a treat after landing, not during the flight. A dragon that gets a treat
in the air learns to stop in the air for the next one, which is how
Puddlewyrms end up hovering over bakeries.

## Landing fields

There are 12 marked landing fields within a day's flight, shown on the free
map we give you at the roost. Landing anywhere else earns a 20 gold fine from
the town council, and farmers may ask you to pay for flattened crops.

## Emergencies

If your dragon won't land, blow one long blast on your whistle and hold on.
If it still hasn't landed after 10 minutes, light the red flare in your
saddlebag and our rescue riders will reach you, usually within 45 minutes.
Within 15 miles of the roost we will bring a replacement dragon at no charge.
Further out, we refund the unused part of your hire once you are back.

If you fall off, or anyone is hurt, light the red flare at once and stay where
you are. The rescue riders are led by Tobias, who flies Hazel and carries a
healer's kit. Dragons stay close to a rider who has fallen and will usually
land beside you without being asked.

If your dragon is hurt or unwell, land at the nearest marked field, keep it
calm, and light the red flare. Don't try to fly it home. A dragon that is
coughing smoke from both nostrils for more than an hour is unwell, not grumpy.
