# Meet the dragons

These are all 21 dragons you can hire at Ember & Scale, by name: 8
Puddlewyrms, 7 Ridgebacks, 4 Thunderwings and 2 Mistgliders. The roost is also
home to the two hatchlings, Pip and Kettle, and to Grandmother Ash, who is
retired. None of those three can be hired.

## Puddlewyrms

Dumpling is our oldest Puddlewyrm and has taught more first-time riders to fly
than any keeper. Dumpling hums while flying, gets slower when the humming
stops, and has never once been in a hurry. Dumpling gives the free short hops
on Dragon Day.

Lady Pemberton is a Puddlewyrm who answers only to her full name. Riders who
call her "Pem" will find she has decided not to take off. She is otherwise the
calmest dragon at the roost and the one we give to riders who are frightened
of heights.

Toast is a small orange Puddlewyrm who is terrified of pigeons. Toast is a
lovely, gentle flyer, but if a pigeon gets close Toast will land at once,
wherever that is. Toast has landed in two duck ponds and one wedding.

Biscuit is a Puddlewyrm who likes to be told where you are going before take
off, and will sulk if you change your mind halfway. Nettle is a Puddlewyrm who
collects buttons and is the most likely dragon to hoard something of yours.

Crumpet is the Puddlewyrm who sleeps under the floor of the Singed Kettle and
keeps the café warm. Crumpet is only hired in the afternoon, after a long
breakfast, and smells faintly of toast for the whole flight.

Bramble is our shyest Puddlewyrm. Bramble flies best with a quiet rider, hides
behind Dumpling in the paddock, and will not take off while anyone is
clapping. Teasel is our youngest Puddlewyrm and likes to circle every church
spire on the way, though never higher than the top of it.

## Ridgebacks

Gerald is the largest Ridgeback at the roost. Gerald loves watching sheep and
will slow down over any field that has them. Gerald does not eat sheep and is
offended by the suggestion. Gerald is our most requested dragon for weddings.

Hazel is the fastest of our Ridgebacks and a little proud of it. Hazel likes
to overtake other dragons and will need a firm hand on the reins near other
riders. Hazel is the Ridgeback our rescue riders use.

Captain Mopsy is a Ridgeback who was named by a six-year-old and has never
recovered. Captain Mopsy is very good with nervous passengers and very bad
with geese. Brick is a quiet, broad Ridgeback who will carry almost anything
and complain about all of it.

Juniper is the Ridgeback most likely to land in Brightwater Lake, and the best
swimmer at the roost. On a hot day, riders on Juniper should expect to get wet
and should keep anything that matters in a closed saddlebag.

Pepper is a Ridgeback who sneezes more than any dragon at the roost, mostly
in spring. A dragon sneeze counts as fire, so riders who hire Pepper usually
add Fire Cover when they book. Barnaby is a steady Ridgeback who flies the
Coast Run better than anyone and doesn't mind gulls at all.

## Thunderwings

Vortigern is our most dramatic Thunderwing. Vortigern roars on take-off, on
landing, and sometimes in between for no reason anyone has found. Vortigern
crossed the Grey Mountains in under two hours once and has mentioned it ever
since.

Saffron is a Thunderwing who will only land facing east. Riders should allow
an extra minute for Saffron to turn around above the landing field. Saffron is
the calmest of our Thunderwings in wind, though Thunderwings are still never
flown in storms.

Augustine is our most patient Thunderwing and the one we give to groups of
four. Augustine waits for every rider to be strapped in before even thinking
about taking off, and is the only Thunderwing who doesn't roar on landing,
just sighs very loudly.

Cobalt is our youngest Thunderwing, so dark a blue that it looks black. Cobalt
flies the Mountain Crossing more than any other dragon and knows the way to
the Far Beacon without being steered.

## Mistgliders

Whisper and Moth are our two Mistgliders. Whisper is the older and steadier of
the two and is the Mistglider used for Night Flying lessons. Moth is drawn to
lights and must be kept away from the town lanterns, which is why Moth always
flies with an experienced rider.

## Hiring a dragon by name

A named dragon costs the same as any other dragon of its kind. Dumpling, Lady
Pemberton, Toast, Biscuit, Nettle, Crumpet, Bramble and Teasel are
Puddlewyrms: 12 gold an hour or 60 gold for a full day, with a 100 gold
deposit.

Gerald, Hazel, Captain Mopsy, Brick, Juniper, Pepper and Barnaby are
Ridgebacks: 25 gold an hour or 120 gold for a full day, with a 250 gold
deposit.

Vortigern, Saffron, Augustine and Cobalt are Thunderwings: 60 gold an hour or
300 gold for a full day, with an 800 gold deposit and our Advanced Rider
badge. Whisper and Moth are Mistgliders: 35 gold an hour or 120 gold for a
whole night, with a 400 gold deposit.

You can ask for a dragon by name when you book, and we will try, but we can't
promise. A dragon may already be out, resting after a long hire, or simply not
in the mood, and the keepers have the final say.

## Hatchlings

Pip and Kettle are our hatchlings. They are about the size of a large goose,
cannot be ridden, and cannot be hired, bought or adopted. Visitors can meet
them at the roost. Pip likes to be scratched under the chin. Kettle steams
when excited and should not be held near anything paper.

## Retired

Grandmother Ash is the dragon who started it all: the Ridgeback who landed in
Ida Ashryver's turnip field seventy years ago and refused to leave. She is
retired, sleeps most of the day in the warm corner of the old barn, and can be
visited by sponsors. She does not fly any more and does not want to be asked.
