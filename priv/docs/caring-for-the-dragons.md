# How we look after the dragons

## Where the dragons live

The dragons sleep in the long barn at the top of Cinder Hill, each in its own
stall lined with warm stones. Grandmother Ash sleeps in the warm corner of the
old barn next door. The hatchlings Pip and Kettle sleep in a straw nest by the
keepers' hut, where someone can hear them at night.

## Feeding the dragons

The keepers feed the dragons twice a day, at dawn and again once the last
dragon is home. They eat mostly mutton, fish and turnips, and Puddlewyrms eat
a great many apples. Every dragon is fed before every hire, which is why the
only food a rider should give it is the treats in the bag.

No dragon at the roost is fed goat after 6pm, by keepers or anyone else. A
dragon that eats goat late is awake all night, and so is everyone within a
mile.

## Scales, claws and wings

Once a week the keepers oil every dragon's scales with scale oil, which keeps
them supple and stops them squeaking in flight. Claws are trimmed once a
season. Wings are checked for tears after every hire, and a dragon with a torn
wing doesn't fly until it has healed.

## Rest

A dragon back from a hire of 3 days or more rests at the roost for two days
before it is hired again. That is one reason we can't promise a particular
dragon by name. Every dragon also gets a quiet morning after a festival day,
because festival days are tiring for everybody.

## When a dragon is unwell

A dragon that coughs smoke from both nostrils for more than an hour is unwell,
not grumpy. Unwell dragons don't fly. The keepers look after them in the long
barn, and the dragon healer from town visits every week and whenever she is
sent for.

## Growing old

Dragons live for a very long time. Grandmother Ash is more than ninety and
Dumpling is about sixty. When a dragon is too old to fly for hire, it retires
to the roost and stays with us for the rest of its life. Retired dragons are
never sold or given away.
