# Dragons and the town

## The Dragon Charter

The town council's Dragon Charter says a dragon can't be owned by a person,
only looked after. It is why Ember & Scale doesn't sell dragons, and why
nobody can sell you one honestly. Under the Charter, the roost looks after its
dragons and hires them out, and the dragons stay at the roost for life.

The Dragon Charter also says every dragon kept in the county must be
registered with the town council, and that the council inspects every roost
once a year. Ember & Scale has passed every inspection for seventy years. The
inspectors always leave with scorched hats.

## Landing rules

The town council allows dragons to land only at the 12 marked landing fields.
Landing anywhere else earns a 20 gold fine from the council, and farmers may
ask you to pay for flattened crops. The fine is the rider's to pay, not the
dragon's, whoever decided to land.

## Where you can't fly

Never fly over the royal palace, the cathedral or the Duke's orchards. The
Duke's orchards lie between Oldbridge Green and Fairmarket Common, so go round
them to the north. The Duke has a trebuchet and is not afraid to use it.

Griffin couriers carrying the royal post always have right of way. A griffin
with a red satchel is carrying the royal post: give it room and let it pass.
Our search griffins, Pickle, Rook and Marigold, don't carry the post and
don't have right of way, though they act as if they do.

## Getting along with the neighbours

The roost is half a day's walk from town on the Brightwater road. Horses don't
like the smell of dragons, so the Windmill Inn, a mile down the hill, stables
visitors' horses. Shepherds near Shepherd's Top are used to Gerald slowing
down over their sheep and have stopped complaining.
