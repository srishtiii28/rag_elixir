# About the roost

## How it started

Ember & Scale started seventy years ago, when a Ridgeback landed in Ida
Ashryver's turnip field and refused to leave. Ida couldn't move the dragon, so
she started charging people to sit on it. That Ridgeback is Grandmother Ash,
who is now retired and still lives at the roost.

The roost is run today by Ida's granddaughter, Nesta Ashryver, our head
dragonkeeper. Nesta has kept dragons all her life, knows every one of ours by
the sound of its snoring, and is the person to send a raven to with a
complaint.

## The keepers

Nesta Ashryver is the head keeper. Fen runs the riding school. Tobias leads
the rescue riders and flies Hazel. Maud runs bookings and answers every raven.
Six more keepers feed, oil and look after the dragons, and are the people who
will brief you before your flight.

The search griffins, Pickle, Rook and Marigold, look for dragons more than 24
hours late. They are very good at it and very expensive to feed, which is why a
search usually costs 150 gold.

## Finding us

The roost is on Cinder Hill, just past the old windmill, half a day's walk
from town on the Brightwater road. You can see it from the road by the smoke.
Horses don't like the smell of dragons, so stable your horse at the Windmill
Inn, a mile down the hill, and walk up.

## At the roost

The Singed Kettle is our café at the roost. It is always warm, because a
Puddlewyrm sleeps under the floor. It serves tea, toast, soup and cake. Riders
can't take food from the café onto a dragon, because the dragon will get to
it first.

The roost shop sells maps, riding gloves, wooden dragon pins, scale oil and,
after the Great Moult, moulted scales for 2 gold each. It doesn't sell
dragons, dragon eggs, or anything a dragon has hoarded.

## Visiting without riding

You can visit the roost without hiring a dragon. Visiting is free during
opening hours, from dawn until the second bell after sunset. You can watch the
dragons from the viewing wall, meet the hatchlings Pip and Kettle at feeding
time, and eat at the Singed Kettle.
